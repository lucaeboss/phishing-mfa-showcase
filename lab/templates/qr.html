QR-code phishing (“quishing”)

Same captive-portal attack — different delivery. Instead of a link, the victim scans a QR code. Scan the code below with a phone on the same network to land on the training portal.

Why attackers use QR codes

  • The URL is hidden. You can’t read a QR with your eyes, so the usual “check the link before clicking” advice doesn’t apply.
  • It jumps to a phone. Phones are often personal and unmanaged — no corporate URL filtering, smaller screen, address bar easy to miss.
  • It dodges email scanners. A QR embedded in an image or printed on a poster sails past link-scanning that would flag a raw URL.

Defenses

  • Treat a QR that leads to a login as suspicious — navigate to the site yourself instead.
  • Check the full URL on the phone’s preview before opening, and look at the real address bar after.
  • Mobile device management (MDM) and phishing-resistant sign-in (passkeys) still apply on the phone — see Stage 4.

Scope: this page builds a QR for your local lab host only. Don’t encode a real or public site, and run the scan in a controlled, in-person setting.
